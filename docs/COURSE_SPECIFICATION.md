# IELTS Quest — Course Specification

Version: 1.0

---

# 1. Course Goal

IELTS Quest uses stories to help learners gradually master IELTS vocabulary.

The course must follow one core principle:

Easy → Easy+ → Medium → Medium+ → Hard

The first missions should be easy to complete so that learners can build confidence before moving to more difficult vocabulary and sentence structures.

---

# 2. Vocabulary Source

The primary vocabulary source is the IELTS vocabulary material provided by the learner.

Vocabulary should not be added randomly.

Each word should be classified by:

- Chapter
- Topic
- Difficulty
- Part of speech
- Learning priority

Additional supporting words may appear in stories when necessary, but they are not automatically treated as core test words.

---

# 3. Course Structure

The course structure is:

Chapter

↓

↓

# 4. Difficulty System

Difficulty increases gradually across the entire course.

## Level 1 — Easy

Purpose:

Build confidence and restart basic English learning.

Story requirements:

- Short sentences
- Familiar grammar
- Mostly common vocabulary
- Clear storyline
- Limited number of new words

Recommended story length:

80–130 words

Core learning vocabulary:

6–10 words

Quiz vocabulary:

Approximately 6 words

---

## Level 2 — Easy+

Story requirements:

- Slightly longer sentences
- Basic connectors such as because, when, before and after
- More IELTS vocabulary
- Previously learned words begin to reappear

Recommended story length:

120–170 words

Core learning vocabulary:

8–12 words

Quiz vocabulary:

6–8 words

---

## Level 3 — Medium

Story requirements:

- More complex sentence structures
- Increased IELTS vocabulary density
- More descriptive language
- Previously learned vocabulary appears naturally

Recommended story length:

160–220 words

Core learning vocabulary:

10–15 words

Quiz vocabulary:

8–10 words

---

## Level 4 — Medium+

Story requirements:

- Longer sentences
- More academic expressions
- More complex grammar
- Higher vocabulary density

Recommended story length:

200–280 words

Core learning vocabulary:

12–18 words

Quiz vocabulary:

8–12 words

---

## Level 5 — Hard

Purpose:

Prepare learners for IELTS-level reading.

Story requirements:

- IELTS-style vocabulary
- More academic language
- Complex sentence structures
- Higher information density

Recommended story length:

250–350 words

Core learning vocabulary:

15–25 words

Quiz vocabulary:

10–15 words

---

# 5. Difficulty Progression

Difficulty should increase across the whole course rather than restarting from Easy in every topic.

Example:

Mission 1 → Easy

Mission 2 → Easy

Mission 3 → Easy+

Mission 4 → Easy+

Mission 5 → Easy+

Mission 6 → Medium

Mission 7 → Medium

Mission 8 → Medium

Mission 9 → Medium+

Mission 10 → Medium+

Later chapters gradually move toward Hard.

Difficulty may be adjusted after real learner testing.

---

# 6. Story Design Rules

Each Mission contains one main story.

Every story must:

- Have a clear beginning, development and ending.
- Use target vocabulary naturally.
- Avoid unnatural sentences written only to include vocabulary.
- Reuse previously learned vocabulary where appropriate.
- Match the current difficulty level.
- Avoid introducing too many difficult words at once.
- Maintain an understandable storyline.

The earliest stories should deliberately feel easy.

The learner should experience frequent successful completion before difficulty increases.

---

# 7. Vocabulary Display Rules

Vocabulary is divided into two categories.

## Core Learning Words

These are words the learner is expected to remember.

They display:

- Word
- IPA pronunciation
- Part of speech
- Chinese meaning
- English definition
- Example sentence
- Example sentence translation

Core words can be selected for quizzes.

## Supporting Words

These are useful words that may still be unfamiliar to the learner but are not current test targets.

Examples may include words such as:

solid

interact

gentle

surface

Supporting words:

- Can be clicked for explanation
- Do not need the same visual underline as core learning words
- Are normally excluded from the current Mission quiz

This prevents the page from becoming visually crowded while still supporting learners who have not used English for a long time.

---

# 8. Translation Rules

During the initial Study stage:

Chinese sentence translations should not be fully displayed by default.

After the learner submits the quiz:

Sentence-by-sentence Chinese translations are unlocked.

Format:

English sentence

↓

Corresponding Chinese translation

This allows learners to attempt comprehension independently before seeing the translation.

---

# 9. Quiz Rules

Each Mission generates a vocabulary quiz.

Quiz requirements:

- Questions are based primarily on core learning words.
- Question order can change between attempts.
- The learner types answers into editable input boxes.
- Answers are checked after submission.
- Correct answers are displayed.
- The learner can modify incorrect answers and submit again.
- The system calculates accuracy.

The same Mission may generate different blank positions during later attempts.

---

# 10. Result Rules

After submission, display:

- Accuracy percentage
- Correct answers
- Incorrect answers
- Sentence translations
- Retry option
- Finish button

Example:

Accuracy: 83%

Correct: 5 / 6

---

# 11. Finish Behaviour

The Result page must include a Finish button.

When the learner clicks Finish:

Return to the Mission's initial page.

The learning history must NOT be deleted.

The Mission should retain:

- Completion status
- Best score
- Attempt count

The learner should not need to repeatedly use the browser Back button to leave a completed Mission.

---

# 12. Vocabulary Repetition

Vocabulary should not disappear after one Mission.

Previously learned words should naturally reappear in later stories.

Recommended repetition pattern:

First appearance → direct learning

Second appearance → story review

Third appearance → contextual reinforcement

Later appearances → natural long-term review

The exact repetition algorithm can be improved after learner testing.

---

# 13. Mission Completion

A Mission is considered completed after the learner:

1. Reads the Study content.
2. Completes the Quiz.
3. Submits answers at least once.
4. Reaches the Result stage.
5. Clicks Finish.

A perfect score is NOT required to finish a Mission.

The learner may retry until reaching 100%.

---

# 14. Learning Progress

The system should eventually record:

- Completed Missions
- Best score
- Number of attempts
- Learned vocabulary
- Words requiring review
- Chapter progress

MVP data may initially be stored locally in the browser.

Cloud synchronization can be added in a later version.

---

# 15. MVP Course Content

The first usable version does not require the entire vocabulary book.

Development should begin with a small set of Missions.

Initial target:

Mission 1

Mission 2

Mission 3

After these Missions are tested successfully, additional Missions will be added progressively.

This allows software development and IELTS study to happen at the same time.

---

# 16. Core Development Principle

IELTS Quest follows:

Build → Learn → Test → Improve → Expand

The learner should receive usable learning content before the entire course is completed.
